# Word-Finding Difficulty Remediation Strategies

Evidence-based techniques for reducing tip-of-the-tongue (TOT) episodes and improving real-time word retrieval.

## Clinical Pathways

Two distinct retrieval pathways from speech-language pathology research:

**Semantic strategies** (meaning-based): Categorization, describing attributes, word-webbing, fill-in-the-blank associations ("A pair of ___"). Wright et al. (1983) found highly significant improvement in picture naming.

**Phonological strategies** (sound-based): Syllabification, sound segmentation, rhyming, imagery. Wing (1990) found significant improvement in picture naming with phonological treatment.

## Acute TOT Relief

Counterintuitively, the best immediate strategy is to **stop searching mentally**. Effort floods working memory with semantically related competitors that block the target word's phonological pathway (NeuroLaunch). Instead: describe the concept, name a related word, or return later.

## Lifestyle Factors

Anxiety and fatigue measurably worsen TOT frequency. Box breathing (4-4-4), intentional pausing, and adequate sleep reduce episodes. Spaced retrieval — revisiting new words within 24 hours — converts passive vocabulary to active use.
